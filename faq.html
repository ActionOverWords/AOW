Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about what this site does, how promises get verified, how we try to stay nonpartisan, and what to do if something looks wrong.

What is ActionOverWords Index?+

ActionOverWords Index (AOW Index) is an independent tracker of specific promises made by elected officials — federal and state — and what actually happened to them: kept, broken, or somewhere in between. Most political coverage tracks what officials say. This tracks what happened to what they said, with every entry linked back to where it came from.

How is each promise verified before you assign it a status?+

Every promise on this site is checked against three sources before a status is set: one official government source (a vote record, a signed bill, an agency filing, or similar primary record), one mainstream/left-leaning news source, and one right-leaning news source. A status only gets applied once it holds up across all three — not just one outlet's version of events. All three sources are linked directly on the promise, so you can check the underlying reporting yourself instead of taking our word for it.

How do you keep the tracker from favoring one side?+

The same six statuses and the same AOW Score formula apply to every official, regardless of party — a status is earned by what happened, not by who's being covered. A party badge (Democrat, Republican, Independent, Nonpartisan) is shown the way an election map shows one: as a fact, not a verdict, and it's never assumed from context. The three-source rule above exists specifically so a status can't rest on reporting from only one side of the aisle.

What do the statuses and the AOW Score actually mean?+

Every promise gets one of six statuses — Not Started, In Progress, Stalled, Compromise, Completed, or Broken — based on what's actually happened so far, not on intent. The AOW Score is a 0–100 number for each official: the average of their tracked promises weighted by status (Completed = 100, Compromise = 60, In Progress = 40, Stalled = 15, Not Started and Broken = 0). It's a fixed, published formula, not a case-by-case editorial call — anyone can recompute it from an official's own promise list and get the same number. Full definitions and worked examples are on the About tab.

I think a status is wrong, or I want to suggest a promise to track. What do I do?+

Use the Contact form on the Index and choose “Suggested correction” or “Appeal a status.” Appeals need a named source — the outlet and headline you're citing — so it can be checked the same way every other entry gets checked. Every submission is reviewed; it's how the record gets more accurate over time.

Still have a question?

General questions, corrections, and status appeals all go through the same Contact form, on the About tab back on the Index.
