Editorial content, sourcing & disclaimer

The heart of this site is a set of judgment calls: has a given promise been kept, broken, or something in between? We take that seriously, and we try to be transparent about how we get there.

Every promise is checked against three sources before we assign it a status: one official government source (a vote record, a signed bill, an agency filing, or similar primary record), one mainstream/left-leaning news source, and one right-leaning news source. All three are linked directly on the promise, so you can check the underlying reporting instead of taking our word for it. Full methodology is on the About tab and in our FAQ.

Even so, a promise's status, its category, any note attached to it, and the AOW Score are our editorial judgment applied to those sources — not a government record, not a court finding, not a certified fact, and not an accusation of wrongdoing. They reflect our good-faith reading of publicly reported information about a public official's actions in their official or campaign capacity, as of the date shown. We may have it wrong; sources get updated, retracted, or reported differently by different outlets, and a status that was accurate when set can become outdated as events unfold. That's what the correction and appeal process described below is for.

Nothing on this site is legal, financial, or investment advice, and nothing here should be treated as a comprehensive record of an official's conduct — we cover specific tracked promises, not everything an official has ever said or done.
